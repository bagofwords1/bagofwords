## KQ1. Current frontier and mid-tier models: release dates, API prices, context windows, data-relevant benchmark scores

### Takeaway
The frontier in October 2026 is Claude Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 / Fable 5.1 (Anthropic), GPT-6 Astra / GPT-6.1 Sol / GPT-5.6 Sol (OpenAI), Gemini 4 Argon (limited availability) and Gemini 3.x Flash/Pro (Google), with Muse Spark 1.3 (Meta), Grok 4.7 (xAI), Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Qwen3.8 Max close behind. Official per-token prices now span ~two orders of magnitude ($0.10/$0.50 for Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna up to $10/$50 for Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra), every frontier vendor sells a 1M-token context, and no public leaderboard reports a single clean "bare model, same harness" number for text-to-SQL at the frontier; per-model SQL numbers exist mostly through harness-specific entries.

## KQ2. Open-weight models competitive for text-to-SQL ("cheap model + good harness")

### Takeaway
There is solid evidence that (a) harness quality moves text-to-SQL scores far more than base-model choice, and (b) cheap open-weight models (GLM-5.x, DeepSeek V4.x Flash, Qwen3-Coder, MiniMax M3, Kimi K2.x) sit within a few points of mid-tier proprietary models on data-agent tasks at 3–10x lower cost — but specialized small text-to-SQL fine-tunes (Arctic-Text2SQL-R1, XiYanSQL-QwenCoder) are 2025-era, BIRD-centric, and have not been shown on Spider 2.0-class enterprise tasks.

## KQ4. Evidence on model routing / auto model selection

### Takeaway
Public evidence that routing saves money is real but narrower than marketing suggests: the rigorous results (RouteLLM; Anthropic's own measured multi-model experiments) show large savings only when (a) a real capability gap exists and (b) the cheap model handles the bulk of turns; Anthropic's own docs conclude "tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models" and that most workloads end after an effort sweep.

### Cited Findings
- RouteLLM (LMSYS/Berkeley, July 2024): routers trained on Chatbot Arena preference data; vs GPT-4-only, cost reductions "over 85% on MT Bench, 45% on MMLU, and 35% on GSM8K ... while still achieving 95% of GPT-4's performance"; strong model GPT-4 Turbo, weak model Mixtral 8x7B; savings counted as reduction in GPT-4 calls vs a random router; generalized without retraining to Claude 3 Opus / Llama 3 8B; "over 40% cheaper" than commercial routers (Martian, Unify) at matched performance — [LMSYS RouteLLM](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-07-01-routellm/), [arXiv 2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665v2)
- Not Diamond: "on MMLU Not Diamond beats GPT-4o while also lowering cost by nearly 30%", with routers "custom-trained for each benchmark dataset" and the concession that benchmarks are weakly correlated with real-world performance; internal case study "saved 51% in inference costs, representing $750,000 in savings on an annualized basis" (self-reported) — [Not Diamond benchmarks](https://docs.notdiamond.ai/docs/benchmark-performance), [Not Diamond blog](https://www.notdiamond.ai/blog/using-not-diamond-to-reduce)
- Anthropic "Optimizing for cost and intelligence" (internal, "directional" measurements): "Sweep effort on your current model. Most workloads end here." — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Anthropic advisor pattern results: Haiku 5.5 or Sonnet 5.5 executor with Opus 5.5 advisor consulted "0 of 198 questions" on GPQA Diamond, yet the advisor tool definition added 12% (Haiku) / 25% (Sonnet) to cost per question; Opus 5.5 `high` + Fable 5.1 advisor scored 90.1% at $2.92/attempt vs Opus 5.5 alone at `xhigh` 91.1% at $4.11 ("The advisor buys about what more effort buys"); Chartography: Opus 5.5 `low` + Fable 5.1 advisor consulted 1 of 300 tasks and scored 7 points below Opus 5.5 alone — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Anthropic orchestrator pattern results: on 10 easy BrowseComp problems a Fable 5 coordinator + Sonnet 5 worker cost about half of Fable 5 alone on average and a third at p90 ($12 vs $33), and "The solo model's most expensive run ($84) was also wrong"; on the full harder set "Fable 5 alone matched the coordinator's accuracy at 22% to 30% lower cost"; on a 21.6M-token corpus benchmark, Fable 5.1 lead + 25 Sonnet 5 workers cost 47–55% less than solo but scored 10–12 points lower; "DeepWideSearch: `low` effort matched an orchestrator with a Sonnet 5 worker at 29% lower cost. Lowering effort beat the architecture change." — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Anthropic's decision procedure: "Price the advisor's model alone at low effort first"; "Measure the consult rate"; "Recheck at every model release, since releases shift both the capability gap and the price ratio"; "Measure on your own workload with outcome checks, cost per completed task, and a score-vs-spend curve" — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Anthropic "Choosing a model": "Several Claude models support an effort parameter that trades intelligence for latency and cost within a single model. Tuning effort is often a better lever than switching models."; two starting strategies (efficiency-first with Haiku 5.5; capability-first with Opus 5.5 then "downgrading models over time"); Haiku 5.5 positioned for "sub-agent tasks" — [Anthropic choosing a model](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model)
- Anthropic pricing page lists "Use appropriate models: Choose Haiku for simple tasks, Sonnet for most production workloads, and Opus for the most complex reasoning" as the first cost-optimization strategy — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol launch is itself a routing argument: "matches Astra at roughly one-fifth the cost" on DeepSWE v1.1 (vendor claim) — secondary: [eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/gpt-6-1-sol)
- AA cost-per-task spread across effort levels for one model: Opus 5.5 from $5.98 (max, index 58) to $1.34 (medium, index 51); GPT-6.1 Sol from $0.72 (max, 52) to $0.21 (medium, 48) — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)
- OpenRouter's "Auto Exacto" routing re-evaluates providers roughly every five minutes using throughput, tool-call telemetry and controlled benchmarks; session-based routing keeps provider affinity for cache efficiency — secondary: [MintMCP review](https://www.mintmcp.com/blog/Openrouter-review); OpenRouter + a16z 2025 State of AI report drew on >100 trillion tokens — [OpenRouter blog](https://openrouter.ai/blog/insights/the-2025-state-of-ai-report/)
- Partner marketing claims (Dagster/Not Diamond: "enhancing accuracy by up to 25% and reducing both inference costs and latency by a factor of 10") carry no methodology — [Dagster](https://dagster.io/customers/routing-llm-prompts-with-not-diamond)

### Inferences
- The strongest publicly defensible routing claims are: (1) large savings on easy/routine traffic with negligible quality loss (RouteLLM's 85% on MT Bench; Anthropic's 50% average / 67% p90 on easy BrowseComp); (2) tail-cost insurance (the $84 wrong solo run); (3) savings reverse on hard tasks. BOW's auto-routing story should be framed as "route by difficulty, with escalation", and reported as a score-vs-spend curve, not a single savings %.
- Within-model effort control is now a first-class competitor to cross-model routing (Anthropic, OpenAI and AA all expose effort tiers); a BOW leaderboard that only varies the model and not the effort will be criticized as incomplete.
- Independent, workload-level replications of commercial routers are absent; BOW should not cite Not Diamond / Martian figures as independent evidence.

## KQ5. Known pitfalls in cross-model comparisons and how reputable reports disclose them

### Takeaway
The pitfalls documented in 2026 sources are concrete and quantified: reasoning-effort settings move a single model's score by 7+ index points and its cost 4x; tokenizer changes (~30% more tokens on Claude 4.7+) and tiered/promotional/off-peak pricing break per-token cost comparisons; native vs prompt-based tool calling can halve a model's score; batch-size-dependent kernels make outputs nondeterministic even at temperature 0; and the SQL benchmarks themselves have >50% annotation error rates. Reputable reports disclose effort per row, harness per row, repeats and CIs, temperature, token-count source, and date.

### Cited Findings
- Effort sensitivity: Claude Opus 5.5 scores 58 (max) / 56 (xhigh) / 54 (high) / 51 (medium) with cost per task $5.98 / $3.46 / $1.82 / $1.34 — [AA leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); on LiveSQLBench GPT-5.5 (low) 33.50 slightly beat GPT-5.5 (xhigh) 33.33 — [LiveSQLBench](https://livesqlbench.ai/); Anthropic: on Chartography the advisor configuration at `low` effort scored 7 points below the same model alone — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Disclosure practice for effort: AA lists each effort level as its own model entry ("Claude Sonnet 5 (Adaptive Reasoning, Low/Medium/High/Xhigh Effort)") — [AA model pages](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-sonnet-5-medium); Scale Labs appends effort and harness to the row ("Opus 5 (Claude Code) xhigh") — [Scale Labs](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard); Anthropic's effort defaults differ by model (`high` on Fable 5.1 / Sonnet 5.5, `medium` on Opus 5.5 / Haiku 5.5) so "default settings" are not comparable across models — [Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)
- Tokenizer confound: "Claude 4.7 and later models ... produces approximately 30% more tokens for the same text" — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); AA's performance benchmarking counts tokens client-side with o200k_base while its cost calculation uses provider-reported counts — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking)
- Pricing-structure confounds: Gemini 3.1 Pro re-rates the whole request above 200k tokens ($2→$4 input) — [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); OpenAI >272K long-context tier ($10→$20 input on gpt-6-astra) — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); xAI applies the ≥200k rate "to all tokens in that request" — [xAI models](https://docs.x.ai/docs/models); Haiku 5.5 is priced by prompt length with a 100k threshold — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); DeepSeek off-peak halves prices — [DeepSeek pricing](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/quick_start/pricing)
- Promotional / time-varying prices: GPT-5.6 Sol promo "at least through November 21, 2026" — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); Gemini 3.6–3.8 Flash doubling on January 1, 2027 — [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); Gemini 4 Argon $2/$10 intro → $4/$20 — secondary: [fourweekmba](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-gemini-4-argon-introductory-price-footnote-4-20/); Sonnet 5 intro price later made permanent — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- Caching confounds: cache-read multipliers differ by model (0.025x Fable 5.1, 0.05x Opus/Sonnet 5.5, 0.1x others) — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); AA uses "live measurements of typical cache usage" rather than eval-run cache rates — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking); Anthropic's orchestrator case read ~560M cached tokens vs ~365M solo and still cost half — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Tool-use overhead differs per model: the tool-use system prompt costs 286 tokens on Opus 5.5 / Sonnet 5.5 / Haiku 5.5 but 675 on Opus 4.7 and 497 on Opus 4.6; `tool_choice: any/tool` adds more — [Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)
- Tool-calling format pitfall: in MCPVerse, moving Claude-4-Sonnet from native function calling to prompt-based tool schemas dropped Oracle mode 62.28→35.55 and Standard mode 62.36→15.10, attributed to call-template mismatch and >70% hallucination — [arXiv 2508.16260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16260); some small models score higher with naive prompt serialization than with their trained parser — [arXiv 2605.07731](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07731); models without native tool support fail on "malformed call structures, stray Markdown wrappers, and serialization problems", mixing format compliance into task scores — [arXiv 2512.22673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22673); the serving stack should be treated as an experimental variable because BFCL "reports aggregate accuracy and does not isolate per-model request handling in the serving layer" — [arXiv 2609.26693](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.26693)
- Benchmark validity: an audit of four tool-calling benchmarks found brittle state comparisons, exact-match constraints, incorrect ground truth, trajectory lock-in, and for LLM-judged ones "stochastic rubric generation, judge variance, and hallucinated evaluations" — [arXiv 2607.02577](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02577); text-to-SQL: 52.8% (BIRD Mini-Dev) and 62.8% (Spider 2.0-Snow) annotation error rates, rankings shift up to 9 positions after correction — [arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778)
- Nondeterminism: Thinking Machines (Sep 2025) shows the same prompt in a batch of 1 vs 32 yields different logits because reduction kernels split work by batch size, so hosted inference is nondeterministic even at temperature 0; fix is batch-invariant kernels — [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/defeating-nondeterminism-in-llm-inference); repeated-sampling ("RS@k") is the standard mitigation — [arXiv 2512.14917](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14917)
- Disclosure of repeats/CIs: Epoch runs most models 16 times on GPQA Diamond / Mock AIME, 8 on MATH L5, plots ±1 SE — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks); AA reports index CI <±1% from >10 repeats but per-eval repeats of 1–5 — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking); Scale Labs shows ± on most scores (some rows, e.g. RLI, show none) — [Scale Labs](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard)
- Prompt/sampling disclosure: Epoch uses API-default temperature, zero-shot CoT, empty system prompt and strict answer formatting — [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks); AA uses temperature 0 (non-reasoning) / 0.6 (reasoning), 16,384 output cap for non-reasoning, up to 30 retries on API failure — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking)
- Harness confound in SQL boards: Spider 2.0 rows are "Method + Model" with no per-row dates or verification flags, and the same model (Claude Sonnet 4.5) scores 67.28–90.49 depending on method — [Spider 2.0](https://spider2-sql.github.io/); LiveSQLBench mixes "Model Base" and "Agent" entries in one ranking and several row dates post-date its "Last Updated: 2026-03-02" stamp — [LiveSQLBench](https://livesqlbench.ai/)
- Self-reported vs independent: BenchLM tables are "compiled from provider self-reports and secondary reports ... for display only" — [BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/spider2lite); Gemini-SQL2's 80.04 BIRD score has no public model/API — [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/06/12/google-releases-gemini-sql2-gemini-3-1-pro-text-to-sql-scores-80-04-on-bird-single-model-leaderboard/); AA's methodology page does not state a policy on self-reported scores — [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking)
- Version drift: τ²-bench results "<1.0.1 are not comparable with >=1.0.1" — [tau2-bench](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench); AA index moved v4.1→v4.2→v4.3→v4.3.2 between ~March and October 2026 — [AA v4.2](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/artificial-analysis-intelligence-index-v4-2), [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking)
- Anthropic warns capability gaps and price ratios shift at each release ("Recheck at every model release") — [Anthropic optimizing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence)
- Guidance to report ≥2 tool-use benchmarks because each covers different sub-skills (BFCL: selection/parameters/sequencing; τ-Bench: dialogue + policy compliance) — [benchmarkingagents.com](https://benchmarkingagents.com/best-benchmarks-for-tool-use)

### Inferences
- A BOW eval report will be judged credible if its methodology footer covers: model snapshot IDs and date; effort/reasoning setting per row (and ideally a sweep); native FC vs prompt mode per model and the exact tool schema; temperature and max tokens; number of repeats and ±CI; cost computed from provider-reported usage with cache hit rate stated and price snapshot dated; latency as p50/p90 wall-clock per task (not tokens/s alone); and a reliability column separating format/tool-call failures from wrong answers.
- Because per-token prices, tokenizers and caching all differ, BOW should present cost strictly as "$ per completed task" (AA/Vals convention) and show the price snapshot date, not per-token rates, in the headline chart.
- Given >50% annotation error rates in BIRD/Spider 2.0, BOW's own validated task set (with execution-based or test-case-based grading, as LiveSQLBench does) is more defensible than re-running public SQL benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No single published "reporting checklist" standard for LLM evals was found; the checklist above is synthesized from the cited practices.
- The ~34% performance overhead figure for batch-invariant kernels is a third-party claim not verified against the Thinking Machines post.
- The remainder of the AA methodology page (characters 100k+) and Anthropic optimizing page (characters 100k+) were not read; they may contain further disclosure details (e.g., AA's policy on self-reported scores).
