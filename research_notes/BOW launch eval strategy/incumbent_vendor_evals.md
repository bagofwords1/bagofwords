# Incumbent Vendor Evals: How Data-Platform Vendors Benchmark Their "AI Analyst" Products (as of 2026-10-08)

Scope note: This covers what Databricks, Snowflake, Google, Microsoft, Salesforce/Tableau, ThoughtSpot, AtScale, dbt Labs, Cube, Tinybird, MotherDuck, ClickHouse, Oracle, SAP and Teradata have *publicly* published about the accuracy of their text-to-SQL / conversational-BI products, plus the third-party and academic material that exists around them. Every number below is tagged as either a **vendor-internal claim** or a **third-party / reproducible measurement**. Where no primary source could be reached, that is flagged.

---

## Key Question 1: What exact accuracy figures and benchmarks has each vendor published for its AI analyst (dates and URLs)?

### Takeaway
Every major incumbent that publishes a number does so on its own private question set (28 to 1,533 questions), against an anonymized or straw-man baseline (usually "a frontier coding agent" or "single-shot GPT-4o"), with no public harness. The only vendor-run results on a *public* dataset are ThoughtSpot's BIRD run (82.8%, competitors anonymized, protocol "available on request"), Snowflake/Databricks model-training papers on BIRD (not product results), and Snowflake AI Research entries on Spider 2.0 (Arctic-FLEX 75.14 on Snow track). Google, Microsoft, Tableau, Oracle, SAP and Teradata publish **no** product-level accuracy figure at all.

### Inferences
- The incumbents have converged on the same rhetorical template: "frontier coding agent with raw MCP/SQL access ≈ 20-30% → our product with our context layer ≈ 85-90%+" (Snowflake 23%→47%→83% and 24.1%→86.3%; Databricks 32%→90%+ and 25%/52.4%→84.5%). These are structurally the same claim with no shared dataset, so they cannot be ranked against each other.
- Only ThoughtSpot has put a product (not a model) on a public dataset (BIRD dev) and it still anonymized rivals and withheld the protocol; this suggests the market norm is "public dataset, private protocol."
- Vendors with model-research teams (Snowflake, Databricks, Google) use BIRD/Spider 2.0 to showcase *models*, then explicitly disclaim that those benchmarks represent their *products* (Databricks: BIRD "neither fully captures ... the full-breadth of real products like Databricks AI/BI Genie").
- Google and Microsoft appear to have made a deliberate choice to publish evaluation *tooling* (Fabric SDK, ADK/GenAI Eval codelabs, Discovery Bench) rather than any product accuracy number.

- Could not reach Google Research's primary Gemini-SQL2 announcement; the 80.04% figure is from secondary coverage with conflicting competitor figures (Claude Opus 4.6 at 70.9% vs 70.1%) and conflicting dates (June 3/12/13, 2026).

## Key Question 2: Which vendors claim "semantic layer / context / instructions raise accuracy from X% to Y%", and what is the evidence quality?

### Takeaway
Every vendor with a context product makes this claim; the evidence ranges from fully reproducible (dbt: 11 questions, public repo and data; AtScale 2024: 40 questions, methodology described) to entirely opaque (Google "as much as two thirds", Snowflake Cortex Sense 24.1%→86.3%, Databricks Genie Ontology). All measured deltas are large (+40 to +70 points) but on tiny question sets (5-40 questions) and almost always scored by the vendor selling the context layer.

## Key Question 5: What gaps could an open-source challenger exploit?

### Takeaway
No incumbent publishes: (1) multi-turn/conversation accuracy (Snowflake explicitly says its eval tool doesn't support it), (2) dashboard/visualization-building quality, (3) cost per correct answer on a shared dataset (only ClickHouse and Cortex Sense give any $ figure), (4) latency distributions (only dbt, Tinybird, ClickHouse report latency), (5) governance/RBAC leakage tests, (6) consistency across paraphrases, or (7) the question set behind any product claim. A challenger that ships a public, versioned, runnable eval on these axes against a cleaned public dataset would be measuring things no incumbent has ever disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- **Multi-turn:** Snowflake Cortex Analyst evaluations: "Evaluation queries are processed independently. Follow-up or multi-turn conversation evaluation is not supported." — [Snowflake docs](https://docs.snowflake.com/user-guide/snowflake-cortex/cortex-analyst-evaluations); Databricks: "Benchmark questions run as new conversations. They do not carry the same context as a threaded Genie conversation." — [Databricks docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/genie-agents/monitor)
- **Consistency / paraphrase robustness:** Databricks recommends "between two and four phrasings of the same question" in benchmarks but publishes no consistency metric — [Databricks docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/genie-agents/monitor); practitioners report Genie "rephrasing the same question could produce different SQL and different answers" — [Apex Systems](https://www.apexsystems.com/insights/article/how-do-we-make-databricks-genie-reliable-enough); Microsoft notes Copilot outputs are nondeterministic — [Microsoft Learn tutorial](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-bi/create-reports/tutorial-copilot-power-bi-prepare-model); TechInformed on Spotter Semantics: no "benchmarks comparing answer consistency, accuracy or cost." — [TechInformed](https://techinformed.com/thoughtspot-launches-spotter-semantics-for-more-consistent-ai-analytics/)
- **Cost per answer:** Only public figures: Cortex Sense "$1.76 to $0.59 per query" (internal, June 2026) — [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/enterprise-ai-agents-grounded-context/); ClickHouse full-run cost "$52" (Fable 5.1) vs "$1" (DeepSeek V4 Flash) for 201 questions — [ClickHouse](https://clickhouse.com/blog/agentic-analytics-benchmark-data-agent-mnist); Bridgewater $0.035 vs $0.56 per BIRD task — [Bridgewater](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs/putting-task-expertise-into-rl-to-achieves-state-of-the-art-on-text-to-sql). No BI vendor publishes cost-per-correct-answer. Modelwire on Gemini-SQL2: announcement "says nothing about latency, cost per query, or how the system handles ambiguous natural language." — [The Model Wire](https://themodelwire.com/article/google-researchs-gemini-sql2-tops-text-to-sql-benchmarks-by-a-wide-margin-01KV0FKVEMB1SACW3NG8BX8ZY3)
- **Latency:** dbt: "GPT models on `xhigh` averaged over 20 seconds per query vs. 8 seconds on `high`" — [dbt](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026); Tinybird: Gemini 2.5 Pro Preview "40s/gen" — [Tinybird](https://www.tinybird.co/blog/which-llm-writes-the-best-sql); Databricks Genie One only relative: "2× faster than the strongest coding agent" — [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/introducing-genie-one-genie-ontology-and-genie-agents). Snowflake's eval tool records latency but publishes none.
- **Query efficiency / data scanned:** Only Tinybird measures rows/bytes read vs human baseline ("Human SQL reads ~31M rows and just 760MB per query"; Scout "129MB/query"). — [Tinybird](https://www.tinybird.co/blog/which-llm-writes-the-best-sql)
- **Dashboard / visualization quality:** No vendor publishes any chart or dashboard-generation accuracy. The only related numbers are academic (Text2Vis pass rates 26% and 42%, nvBench 2.0) and "not tests of Tableau." — [BTW Media](https://btw.media/en/tableau-can-build-the-chart-the-hard-part-is-making-the-question-safe)
- **Ambiguity handling / abstention:** Google is the only vendor measuring ambiguity sweeps (F1 1.00 → 0.00 on one case) and only for table discovery — [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/evaluate-agent-performance); data.world measured an "I don't know" rate (8%) in 2023 — [data.world](https://data.world/mstatic/assets/pdf/kg_llm_accuracy_benchmark_11132023_public.pdf); Databricks marketing claims Genie "proactively seeks clarification" but publishes no abstention metric — [Databricks answers page](https://answers.databricks.com/best-ai-first-bi-platform-2026).
- **Governance / RBAC tests:** No vendor publishes any accuracy-under-row-level-security or data-leakage eval; Databricks notes "Unity Catalog governs which tables and columns a Genie Agent can query, but it does not resolve business vocabulary." — [developers.databricks.com](https://developers.databricks.com/perspectives/ai-text-to-sql-is-accurate-when-it-is-grounded-and-evaluated)
- **Answer-correctness vs SQL-executes:** Typedef: neither Cortex Sense nor Genie Ontology checks "whether the computed number is valid" — [Typedef](https://www.typedef.ai/blog/cortex-sense-vs-genie-ontology-which-one-checks-the-answer); Databricks: a query "can run cleanly yet use the wrong date field or revenue definition." — [developers.databricks.com](https://developers.databricks.com/perspectives/ai-text-to-sql-is-accurate-when-it-is-grounded-and-evaluated)
- **Benchmark hygiene:** BIRD Mini-Dev 52.8% error rate (Bridgewater, Aug 2026); Spider 2.0-Snow only 120/547 public gold answers; ThoughtSpot had to skip BIRD's official scorer. — [Bridgewater](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs/putting-task-expertise-into-rl-to-achieves-state-of-the-art-on-text-to-sql); [arXiv 2609.04641](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04641); [ThoughtSpot](https://www.thoughtspot.com/blog/ai-analyst-accuracy-bird-benchmark)
- **Statistical rigor:** Only ThoughtSpot reports a confidence interval ("±6 points" on 231 questions) and admits a margin "has not yet cleared a paired per-question test." Databricks (13, 28 questions), Snowflake (150, undisclosed), dbt (11), MotherDuck (40), AtScale (40, 5) all publish point estimates without CIs. — [ThoughtSpot](https://www.thoughtspot.com/blog/ai-analyst-accuracy-bird-benchmark)
- **Baseline honesty:** Incumbents benchmark against "coding agent + raw MCP" (23-32%) while the public Spider 2.0-Snow leaderboard shows third-party agents at 94-97% with no vendor semantic layer. — [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/snowflake-cowork-personal-work-agent/); [Databricks](https://www.databricks.com/blog/pushing-frontier-data-agents-genie); [spider2-sql.github.io](https://spider2-sql.github.io/)

### Inferences
- The highest-leverage gap is *comparability*: a challenger that runs its own product **and** Genie, Cortex Agent, Looker CA, Fabric data agent, and ThoughtSpot on the *same* public question set (with the harness and raw outputs published) would produce the first reproducible cross-vendor number in this category — none of the ~10 vendor-blog "comparisons" has done this.
- Reporting a 2x2 of (accuracy, cost per correct answer) and (first-turn, multi-turn) on a verified subset of Spider 2.0-Snow or a cleaned BIRD slice would directly address three gaps (cost, multi-turn, benchmark hygiene) that no incumbent touches.
- A paraphrase-consistency metric (same question, N phrasings, % identical result sets) is cheap to implement and targets the single most-reported practitioner complaint about Genie.
- Because incumbents' eval tooling runs only inside their own platform, a vendor-neutral open eval harness that can target any of them via API would itself be a distribution asset.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies how often incumbents' agents *abstain* or ask clarifying questions vs. answer wrongly; this would need to be measured, not cited.
- No published data on accuracy degradation with schema size (tables/columns) for any vendor product; ThoughtSpot docs only advise keeping models under ~50 columns. — [ThoughtSpot docs](https://docs.thoughtspot.com/cloud/latest/spotter-worksheet-model.html)
- Could not find any vendor publishing accuracy for non-English prompts (Looker CA is English-only per docs); a multilingual eval would be uncontested but the demand signal is unverified.
