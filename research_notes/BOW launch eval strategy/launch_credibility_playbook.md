# Eval-Centric Launches 2024–Oct 2026: What Built or Destroyed Credibility (Playbook Research Notes)

Scope: how startups used benchmark/eval results in product launches (2024–Oct 2026), what made them credible or got them attacked, and what the developer/data community rewards or punishes. Written for Bag of Words' planned eval-centric launch comparing against Databricks Genie, Snowflake Cortex Analyst, Hex, Julius, and raw Claude Code / Codex via MCP, and across LLMs.

Research date: 2026-10-08. Sources are dated and linked inline. Where a fact comes from a secondary summary rather than the primary document, this is flagged.

## Key Question 1: Case studies of eval-centric launches and their reception

### Takeaway
The pattern across coding agents (Devin, Warp, Augment, Refact, Cursor), model releases (Reflection-70B, Llama 4, GPT-5) and data/analytics vendors (Snowflake Cortex Analyst, Databricks Genie, Defog, Julius, Hex) is consistent: launches that disclosed harness, sample, baselines and limitations (Cognition's SWE-bench report, Augment/Refact open-sourcing the agent, Warp on a third-party leaderboard) got durable credit even when the product later disappointed; launches built on internal, unverifiable, or anonymized comparisons (Cortex Analyst's 150-question internal set, Genie One's 28-question anonymized comparison, Cursor Bench, Reflection-70B) were either ignored by the technical community or actively attacked.

### Cited Findings

**Cognition Devin (March 2024) — the disclosure-heavy launch that aged badly on product, not on methodology**
- Cognition's SWE-bench technical report (dated 03.15.24) disclosed: "We evaluated Devin on a randomly chosen 25% of the SWE-benchmark test set (570 out of the 2,294)"; "The example is considered successful if all of the unit tests pass after patching the edit"; 79/570 = 13.86%; "We limit Devin to 45 minutes of runtime"; "We do not give the agent any other user input during the run"; git remote removed so `git pull` fails; baselines were the best unassisted (Claude 2 + BM25, 1.96%) and best assisted (Claude 2, 4.80%), and "we choose the stronger numbers for the baseline comparison." They also disclosed a non-comparable test-driven variant (23% on 100 tasks, "incomparable to other results from SWE-bench"), a contamination caveat ("the baselines we are comparing to (Claude, GPT, Llama, etc.) face similar data contamination issues"), and that they "manually inspected Devin's successful runs" for leakage. Released the adapted harness and Devin's patches at github.com/CognitionAI/devin-swebench-results; did not release full trajectories, model version, retry count or full prompt text. — [Cognition SWE-bench technical report](https://cognition.com/post/swe-bench-technical-report)
- The Decoder (Mar 2024) called the rate "not outstanding, though significantly better than other language models tested on this benchmark," noting the benchmark "does not yet take into account new models such as Claude 3 or GPT-4 Turbo." — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/cognition-unveils-ai-powered-software-developer-devin-for-better-programming/)
- Within weeks, open-source agents narrowed the gap: Princeton's SWE-agent scored 12.29% and AutoCodeRover was then reported as the new SOTA (Pragmatic Engineer, 2024). — [Pragmatic Engineer, The Pulse](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/i/143712983/devin-reversing-ambitious-claims-and-an-open-source-threat)
- The "Internet of Bugs" YouTube critique went through Devin's Upwork demo frame by frame and argued the task was set up and presented misleadingly; the critique targeted the demo, not the SWE-bench number (secondary summaries). — [eesel.ai review roundup](https://www.eesel.ai/en/blog/cognition-ai-reviews); [Changelog News #90 summary](https://go.waylonwalker.com/thought-247/)
- Answer.AI's "Thoughts On A Month With Devin" (Jan 8, 2025; Hamel Husain, Isaac Flath, Johno Whitaker): 20 tasks, 3 succeeded, 14 failed, 3 inconclusive. Quotes: "But that's the problem - it rarely worked." and "Social media excitement and company valuations have minimal relationship to real-world utility." The post cites Cognition's claim that Devin "could resolve 13.86% of real-world GitHub issues end-to-end on the SWE-bench benchmark" and footnotes that a widely shared demo was later debunked. — [Answer.AI](https://www.answer.ai/posts/2025-01-08-devin.html)
- HN launch-thread evidence is thin: the Algolia record for "Devin, the first AI software engineer" (cognition-labs.com) shows only 1 point / 1 comment for that specific submission (2024-03-19); the main discussion was likely under a different URL and was not retrieved. — [HN Algolia search](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Devin%20SWE-bench&tags=story)

**Cursor Composer / Cursor Bench (Oct 2025 – mid 2026) — proprietary, internal benchmark; criticized for exactly that**
- Cursor's Composer post (Oct 29, 2025) describes Cursor Bench as measuring "not just the agent's correctness, but also its adherence to a codebase's existing abstractions and software engineering practices," built from "real agent requests" by Cursor engineers with "hand-curated optimal solutions." Footnote: scores come from "an internal benchmark in the Cursor tool harness"; models are grouped into classes and only the best per class is shown; the footnote concedes GPT-5 and Sonnet 4.5 "both outperform Composer." No run conditions, model versions, prompts, or trial counts are given; the speed claim is stated only as "on our benchmarks." — [Cursor blog: Composer](https://cursor.com/blog/composer)
- Reception: HN commenters objected that Cursor shared little about the benchmark and did not report standard public benchmarks; a Cursor researcher replied that they used internal code and "would also like to see more benchmarks that reflect the day-to-day agentic coding use" (HN thread via third-party mirror; verify wording at news.ycombinator.com). Reviewers labeled the scores "self-reported and not independently verified yet," contrasting Terminal-Bench 2.0 "maintained by the Laude Institute" as "a third-party standard." — [buildfastwithai Composer 2 review](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/cursor-composer-2-review-2026); [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/cursor-composer-benchmarks/?p=22823948)
- By Composer 2.5 (May 2026) Cursor's own chart noted rivals' scores were self-reported ("Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.5 use self-reported scores for public evals") and reported a Terminal-Bench 2.0 score where Composer trailed (69.3% vs GPT-5.5's 82.7%) — flagged in Cursor's own documentation (secondary summary). — [buildfastwithai Composer 2.5 review](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/blogs/cursor-composer-2-5-review-2026)
- HN reach: "Cursor Introduces Composer 2.5" (2026-05-18) 290 points / 225 comments; but "Cursor Composer 2 is just Kimi K2.5 with RL" (2026-03-20) hit 276 points / 168 comments — the community's biggest Composer thread was about an undisclosed base model, not about scores. — [HN Algolia search](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Cursor%20Composer&tags=story)

**Warp on Terminal-Bench (June 25, 2025) — third-party leaderboard, partial disclosure**
- Warp claimed 52% on Terminal-Bench v0.1.1, "over 20% (almost 9 percentage points) ahead of the next top submission." Disclosed: Claude Sonnet 4 primary + Opus 4 planner, ~2% fallback to Gemini 2.5 Pro / GPT-4.1, Terminal-Bench open harness, all permissions set to "Always Allow," simulated user exempt from rate limits. Not disclosed: number of runs, cost. Also admitted "all runs" averaged ~65% test-suite pass versus the 52% headline without fully explaining the gap, and that Opus 4 did not beat Sonnet 4 for them. — [Warp blog](https://www.warp.dev/blog/terminal-bench)
- The benchmark's own site amplified the result: tbench.ai news, "Warp debuts at #1 on Terminal-Bench, resolving 52% of tasks" (Jun 25, 2025). — [tbench.ai news](https://www.tbench.ai/news)

**Factory Droid on Terminal-Bench (2025–2026)**
- A tbench.ai Terminal-Bench 2.0 snapshot dated March 9, 2026 placed Droid + GPT-5.3-Codex second at 77.3% behind a Forge Code entry at 78.4%; Factory maintains its own leaderboards page ("last updated: December 2025" for Terminal Bench). — [tbench.ai leaderboard](https://tbench.ai/leaderboard); [Factory docs leaderboards](https://docs.factory.ai/jp/leaderboards)
- Caveat: Terminal-Bench 1.0, 2.0 and 2.1 have different task sets; scores are agent+model pairs and not comparable across versions. — [tbench.ai 2.1 leaderboard](https://tbench.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench/2.1)

**Augment Code and Refact.ai on SWE-bench Verified (2025) — "open-source the agent" launches**
- Augment (Mar 31, 2025): 65.4% on SWE-bench Verified, Claude Sonnet 3.7 as driver + o1 as ensembler, "off-the-shelf models"; full pipeline open-sourced (augment-swebench-agent, Docker runs, ensembling, evaluation). — [Augment Code blog](https://www.augmentcode.com/blog/1-open-source-agent-on-swe-bench-verified-by-combining-claude-3-7-and-o1)
- A community leaderboard nonetheless tagged "Augment Agent v0" as proprietary, conflicting with the open-source framing — illustrating that labels on third-party boards can drift from the vendor's claim. — [llmrun.dev SWE-bench Verified](https://llmrun.dev/benchmark/swe-bench-verified?show=all)
- Refact.ai: 69.9% (349/500) and 70.4% (352/500) with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, then 74.4% (372/500) with Claude 4 Sonnet (June 2025), explicitly "pass@1: Refact.ai only had one attempt to solve each task correctly"; marketed as "#1 open-source AI Agent on SWE-bench." Scores are self-reported; a dev.to post explains the build. — [Refact.ai blog](https://refact.ai/blog/2025/1-agent-on-swe-bench-verified-using-claude-4-sonnet/); [Refact dev.to](https://dev.to/refact/how-we-built-the-open-source-sota-ai-agent-on-swe-bench-verified-1gml)
- Context that erodes these wins: an audit of 254 public SWE-bench submissions (arXiv 2609.17394, Sept 15, 2026) found "the leading two entries each resolve 396 of 500 instances," "The top ten share 285 successes and 51 failures, leaving 164 instances that distinguish their outcomes," within-model scaffold ranges up to 29.8 points vs an 8.8-point spread among the top thirty, and paired McNemar tests at α=0.05 separating none of the 29 adjacent top-30 pairs. — [arXiv 2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394)

**Kilo Code / KiloBench (June 2026) — reframing around cost per task**
- Kilo's KiloBench (June 8, 2026) runs all 89 Terminal-Bench 2.0 tasks per trial through Kilo's own harness and reports cost per attempt, cost-to-complete (including retries), harness-specific pass rate and "behavioral fingerprints." Argument: headline token prices understate real cost "by 5x to 30x depending on model and task difficulty"; "The cheapest model for a workload isn't the one with the lowest per-token rate." Trial counts and exact cost formulas were not specified. — [Kilo blog](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/kilobench-because-your-benchmark); [kilo.ai/kilobench](https://kilo.ai/kilobench)
- I found no Cline or Kilo entry with a self-reported SWE-bench score; a glossary page grouping Cline in a 45–60% band has no methodology and is not reliable. — [klu.ai glossary](https://klu.ai/glossary/swe-bench-eval)

**Reflection-70B / Matt Shumer (Sept 2024) — the cautionary tale**
- Timeline (Air Street Press, Sept 10, 2024): Sept 5 Shumer announces Reflection 70B (Llama 3.1-70B finetune) claiming >99% GSM8k and that it "beats GPT-4o on every benchmark tested"; training data from Glaive AI, in which Shumer is an investor; VentureBeat republishes the claims; within a day #1 trending on Hugging Face. Sept 7 Artificial Analysis finds the model "appeared to be underperforming the base model." Sept 9 a private API performs better but cannot be verified. Users via OpenRouter find the API is a Claude wrapper switching among Claude, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini and Llama; OpenRouter pulls access. — [Air Street Press](https://press.airstreet.com/p/reflections-on-reflection); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/worlds-best-open-source-model-falls-short-of-promised-performance/)
- Air Street's lesson: promoters could have asked "basic questions about the team, its resources and track record, whether the benchmark scores are plausible, potential conflicts of interest, and whether the claims seem too good to be true"; the CEO of a sub-$5M company claiming "the best model in the world" "stretches credulity a step too far." — [Air Street Press](https://press.airstreet.com/p/reflections-on-reflection)
- Post-mortem (Oct 2024): Glaive founder Sahil Chaudhary reported a bug in the initial code that inflated scores on some benchmarks; Shumer conceded the model "didn't achieve the benchmarks originally reported" (secondary coverage; the original glaive.ai URL now serves a different company's marketing page). — [getcoai](https://getcoai.com/news/the-reflection-70b-saga-continues-with-release-of-training-data-report); [HN record of "Update on Reflection-70B", 82 points, 2024-10-03](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Reflection%2070B&tags=story)
- HN reach: "Reflection 70B, the top open-source model" 234 points / 95 comments (Sept 5); "Confirmed: Reflection 70B's official API is a wrapper for Sonnet 3.5" 250 points / 64 comments (Sept 9) — the debunk out-scored the launch. — [HN Algolia search](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Reflection%2070B&tags=story)

**How frontier labs footnote competitor comparisons**
- Anthropic Claude 4 (May 22, 2025): scaffold described ("a bash tool and a file editor"); "Claude 4 scores cover all 500 problems" while "OpenAI scores were reported on a 477-problem subset"; high-compute numbers explained in three steps ("We sample multiple parallel attempts"; discard patches failing visible regression tests, "no hidden test information is used"; internal scoring model picks best); competitor numbers sourced to named documents (o3 launch post/system card, GPT-4.1 post, Gemini 2.5 Pro model card). — [Anthropic Claude 4](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-4)
- Anthropic's agent-eval guidance (Jan 9, 2026) defines pass@k ("the likelihood that an agent gets at least one correct solution in k attempts") and pass^k ("the probability that all k trials succeed"), and states "LLM-as-judge graders should be closely calibrated with human experts" and "You won't know if your graders are working well unless you read the transcripts and grades from many trials." — [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- OpenAI (via Latent Space, Feb 23, 2026; Mia Glaese and Olivia Watkins): SWE-bench Verified "is effectively saturated and also highly contaminated"; "every frontier model it tested could reproduce gold patches or problem statements from task IDs alone"; of 138 problems analyzed, 49 tests too narrow and 26 too wide; swyx: a group chat where labs take turns bumping scores by "0.1" and a model "0.1% higher" isn't "super convincing at this point"; Opus 4.5→4.6 was "literally a 0.1% down step." — [Latent Space: SWE-bench is dead](https://latent.space/p/swe-bench-dead)

### Inferences
- Technical audiences forgive a modest number with honest framing (Devin's 13.86% with 45-min limit and random 25% sample; Cursor conceding GPT-5/Sonnet 4.5 beat Composer) far more than a large number with opaque framing (Cortex Analyst's 90% on 150 internal questions, Genie One's 84.5% on 28 anonymized-competitor questions).
- For data/analytics specifically, the vendors with the most market power (Snowflake, Databricks) published the least verifiable comparisons and still got coverage because of press reach — a path not available to a startup, which must instead earn attention via reproducibility (Augment/Refact pattern: open-source the agent and the harness).
- Open-sourcing the agent/harness (Augment, Refact, Cognition's partial release) converts a marketing claim into a reproducible artifact; the community then does the verification for free and the result survives later product criticism (Answer.AI attacked Devin the product, not the SWE-bench methodology).
- The two most-upvoted HN threads in this corpus were debunks (Reflection wrapper 250 pts; "Composer 2 is just Kimi K2.5" 276 pts). Non-disclosure (base model, routing) attracts more attention than any score.

## Key Question 2: Which disclosures build credibility

### Takeaway
The community-accepted bar in 2025–2026 is: open harness + open (or at least described) dataset, exact model versions and dates, n trials with confidence intervals (paired tests when comparing systems), pass@1 clearly separated from pass@k/pass^k, cost and latency per task, named competitor configurations with sources, transcripts for failures, and ideally a third-party or public leaderboard run. Anthropic's error-bars paper and agent-eval guide, Terminal-Bench's leaderboard format, Kilo's cost framing, and the Snowflake AUP's "all information necessary to replicate" clause converge on the same list.

### Cited Findings
- Error bars / statistics: Evan Miller (Anthropic), "Adding Error Bars to Evals" (arXiv 2411.00640, Nov 4, 2024) argues evals should be treated as experiments; notes evals are reported with a "highest number is best" mentality and that Chatbot Arena popularized CIs on Elo scores while "error bars remain noticeably absent from traditional question-and-answer evals"; recommends paired comparisons on the same questions rather than overlapping-interval checks. — [arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640)
- Statistical separation is real work: on SWE-bench Verified, exact paired McNemar tests at α=0.05 "separate none of the 29 adjacent Verified top-thirty pairs"; the authors recommend "reporting comparison-set-specific resolution and model-scaffold provenance" and release a five-step audit protocol. — [arXiv 2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394)
- Trials and variance: Anthropic: "Because model outputs vary between runs, we run multiple trials to produce more consistent results"; a task "that passed on one eval run might fail on the next"; "Each trial should be 'isolated' by starting from a clean environment"; shared infra failures mean "these trials are not independent"; "20-50 simple tasks drawn from real failures is a great start." — [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- pass@k vs pass^k: pass^k (from Sierra's τ-bench) is "the probability that all k attempts succeed"; a secondary write-up cites GPT-4o ~61% single-trial on τ-bench retail but pass^8 below 25%; Sierra says "Anthropic's model cards now discuss the pass^k reliability metric." — [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/tau-bench-shaping-development-evaluation-agents); worked example: 75% per-trial → pass@3 ≈ 98%, pass^3 ≈ 42%. — [skills.cat summary](https://skills.cat/skills/danielmiessler/personal-ai-infrastructure/evals)
- Exact pass@1 statement as a credibility signal: Refact.ai: "Refact.ai only had one attempt to solve each task correctly." — [Refact.ai](https://refact.ai/blog/2025/1-agent-on-swe-bench-verified-using-claude-4-sonnet/)
- Scaffold/compute disclosure: Anthropic Claude 4 explains high-compute selection step-by-step and states "no hidden test information is used"; cites sources for each competitor number; notes subset mismatch (500 vs 477). — [Anthropic Claude 4](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-4)
- Settings disclosure: GPT-5 system card: SWE-bench "was run with the default verbosity setting in the API (verbosity = medium). Changes in verbosity can lead to variation in eval performance." — [GPT-5 System Card](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03267); Aider Polyglot leaderboard distinguishes runs "with an increased 32,000 thinking token limit." — [Simon Willison, Feb 25, 2025](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/)
- Minimal, non-model-specific harness: CompileBench authors: "When designing the benchmark we kept our benchmark harness and prompts minimal, avoiding model-specific tweaks." — [via Simon Willison, Sept 22, 2025](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/)
- Cost and latency: Terminal-Bench 2.1 leaderboard columns are Rank, Model, Agent, Resolution Rate (with "whiskers span the 95% confidence interval"), Cost, Tokens. — [tbench.ai 2.1](https://tbench.ai/leaderboard/terminal-bench/2.1); KiloBench reports cost per attempt, cost-to-complete including retries. — [Kilo blog](https://blog.kilo.ai/p/kilobench-because-your-benchmark); Databricks' Genie One coverage led with "about twice as fast as the strongest coding agent" alongside accuracy. — [Polestar](https://www.polestaranalytics.com/partners/databricks/genie)
- Third-party verification: Vals AI "maintains private test sets intended to preserve benchmark integrity," has open-sourced parts of its infra, is "cited in model cards from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and xAI," and raised a $40M Series A at $400M valuation. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/vals-ai); [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/vals-ai-raises-40-million-series-a-at-400-million-valuation-as-revenue-grows-8x/). Artificial Analysis runs a "mystery shopper policy" (accounts not on their domain so labs can't identify eval traffic) and George Cameron warns that once an eval "becomes the thing everyone looks at, models can get better on it without reflecting overall generalized intelligence." — [Latent Space: Artificial Analysis](https://latent.space/p/artificialanalysis). Artificial Analysis was the actor that broke the Reflection-70B story within 48 hours. — [Air Street Press](https://press.airstreet.com/p/reflections-on-reflection)
- Only 1 of 100 models on the llm-stats SWE-bench Verified board carried an independent-verification badge; "scaffolding moves scores by 10–20 percentage points without changing the model." — [Benchmark Literacy guide (secondary)](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/06/17/benchmark-literacy-guide-reading-coding-agent-benchmarks-critically/)
- Public leaderboards took integrity measures: Terminal-Bench "Leaderboard Integrity and Timeouts" (Sept 9, 2025) and "Leaderboard Integrity Update" introducing "new policies to address cheating and reward hacking" (Apr 19, 2026); LMArena released 2,000+ battles and updated policy after Llama 4. — [tbench.ai news](https://www.tbench.ai/news); [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/updates/lmarena-tightens-rules-after-llama-4-incident)
- Transcript/failure review: Anthropic: "Failures should seem fair: it's clear what the agent got wrong and why"; eval scores aren't taken at face value "until someone reads some transcripts." — [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents); Snowflake's post showed five worked questions with each system's output (the most convincing part of an otherwise unverifiable post). — [Snowflake](https://snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi)
- Replicability as a legal condition: Snowflake AUP permits public disclosure of benchmark results only if "the disclosure includes all information necessary to replicate the Test." — [Snowflake AUP](https://www.snowflake.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy/)
- Judge calibration: Hamel Husain: "'correctness' is somewhat subjective, and you must align the model with a human"; "using raw agreement is generally not recommended and can be misleading when classes are imbalanced"; binary beats granular ("assigning scores or more granular ratings is more onerous to manage than binary ratings"). — [hamel.dev, Mar 29, 2024](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/)
- Hamel on difficulty: "If you're passing 100% of your evals, you're likely not challenging your system enough." — [via Simon Willison, Jul 3, 2025](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/)
- Andrew Ng on eval validity: "Whenever a pair of systems A and B contradicts these criteria, that is a sign the eval is in 'error'." — [via Simon Willison, Apr 18, 2025](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/)

### Inferences
- A startup can bundle most of these into one artifact: a repo with the harness, task set, per-task JSONL results per trial, cost/latency logs, pinned model IDs/dates, and a notebook that recomputes CIs and paired tests. That one repo satisfies Snowflake's replication clause, the error-bars standard, and the "open harness" expectation simultaneously.
- Report pass@1 as the headline, pass^k (k=3 or 5) as the reliability metric that matters for BI users (one wrong number destroys trust), and never report pass@k as if it were pass@1.
- Reporting cost and latency per task is now expected (Terminal-Bench, KiloBench) and is a differentiator vs incumbents, who report neither.

## Key Question 3: What gets punished (with critic quotes)

### Takeaway
The community punishes six things hard: undisclosed or anonymized comparisons, benchmark gaming (special variants, prompt tuning, contaminated public sets), self-grading, presentation that overstates (chart crimes, "accuracy" undefined), tiny leads read as rankings, and demos that do not survive hands-on use. Punishment is swift (Reflection unraveled in 4 days) and the debunk often out-reaches the launch.

### Cited Findings
- Undisclosed special variants: Lambert on Llama 4 LMArena: "Sneaky. The results below are fake, and it is a major slight to Meta's community to not release the model they used to create their major marketing push." — [Interconnects, Apr 7, 2025](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/llama-4). LMArena: Meta "should have made it clearer that the experimental model was a customized model to optimize for human preference." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/11/metas-vanilla-maverick-ai-model-ranks-below-rivals-on-a-popular-chat-benchmark)
- Undisclosed base model: HN "Cursor Composer 2 is just Kimi K2.5 with RL" (276 pts, 168 comments, Mar 20, 2026) — the identifier leak drove the largest Composer thread. — [HN Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Cursor%20Composer&tags=story)
- Wrapper fraud: HN "Confirmed: Reflection 70B's official API is a wrapper for Sonnet 3.5" (250 pts); "The AI Hype Fraud of Reflection-Llama-70B" (43 pts). — [HN Algolia](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Reflection%2070B&tags=story)
- Contamination of public benchmarks: "The SWE-Bench Illusion" (arXiv 2506.12286, Jun 2025, rev. Dec 2025): models identify buggy file paths from issue text alone at up to 76% on SWE-bench vs up to 53% off-benchmark; verbatim 5-gram reproduction up to 35% vs 18%; "performance gains on SWE-Bench-Verified may be partially driven by memorization rather than genuine problem-solving." — [arXiv 2506.12286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286). OpenAI (Feb 2026): "every frontier model it tested could reproduce gold patches or problem statements from task IDs alone"; contamination "often happens even without intentional cheating." — [Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/swe-bench-dead)
- Text-to-SQL benchmarks specifically are broken: Jin, Choi, Zhu, Kang (UIUC), "Text-to-SQL Benchmarks are Broken" (CIDR 2026) / "Pervasive Annotation Errors Break Text-to-SQL Benchmarks and Leaderboards" (PVLDB vol 19): annotation error rates of 52.8% (BIRD Mini-Dev) and 62.8–66.1% (Spider 2.0-Snow, figure varies by version); re-scoring 16 open-source BIRD leaderboard agents on corrected data changed scores −7% to +31% relative and ranks by −9 to +9 positions; rankings on uncorrected vs corrected subsets correlate only ρ=0.32; prior SOTA Contextual-SQL dropped from 1st to 7th. — [CIDR 2026](https://vldb.org/cidrdb/2026/text-to-sql-benchmarks-are-broken-an-in-depth-analysis-of-annotation-errors.html); [arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778v1); [Daniel Kang blog](https://ddkang.substack.com/p/pervasive-annotation-errors-break); Pourreza & Rafiei (EMNLP 2023) earlier found reference-matching "fails for various reasons" and "relative performance changes after a re-evaluation." — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2023.emnlp-main.99)
- Original SWE-bench quality: OpenAI's SWE-bench Verified (Aug 2024) was built because many tasks were under-specified or had bad tests (page returned 403; via secondary) and OpenAI's later deep dive found 49/138 tests too narrow, 26 too wide. — [Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/swe-bench-dead); [openai.com (403 at fetch time)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-swe-bench-verified/)
- Self-grading / judge bias: "Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge" (Wataoka, Takahashi, Ri; arXiv 2410.21819, NeurIPS 2024 workshop): "GPT-4 showed a significant self-preference bias"; LLMs favor lower-perplexity (more familiar) text whether or not self-generated. — [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819). Shisa V2 (Jun 2025): "GPT-4 cannot accurately distinguish performance differences among stronger models." — [via Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/). Hamel: "Model-based evaluation is a meta-problem within your larger problem." — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/)
- Generic frameworks: Hamel: "Don't rely on generic evaluation frameworks to measure the quality of your AI." and product evals must "measure something different from general model benchmarks." — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/)
- Vendor-reported scores flagged by default: Simon Willison on GPT-5.1-Codex-Max (Nov 19, 2025): "OpenAI's self-reported SWE-Bench Verified score is particularly notable"; on Opus 4.1 (Aug 5, 2025): "Anthropic's own benchmarks show very small incremental gains." — [simonwillison.net/tags/evals](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/)
- Tiny leads read as rankings: swyx: labs bumping scores by "0.1" is not "super convincing"; arXiv 2609.17394: "Small differences on coding-agent leaderboards are often read as an ordering of systems." — [Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/swe-bench-dead); [arXiv 2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394)
- Mollick (Nov 2025, "Giving your AI a Job Interview"): benchmarks' public answer keys leak into training; on MMLU-Pro items about Homo erectus cranial capacity and a 1979 Cheap Trick album, "I have no idea" what getting them right tells us; "vibe benchmarking" gives limited insight, so interview/test the model on your real tasks. — [One Useful Thing summary](https://prismix.dev/news/05699876a0b2); [SUNY summary](https://emtech.suny.edu/?p=29441)
- Hands-on contradiction of claims: Answer.AI on Devin: "it rarely worked" after 20 tasks (3 successes). — [Answer.AI](https://www.answer.ai/posts/2025-01-08-devin.html)
- Overfitting to the leaderboard: Lambert: "We've seen many open models that come around to maximize on ChatBotArena" at the expense of math/code; Artificial Analysis' Cameron: evals that everyone watches get optimized "without reflecting overall generalized intelligence." — [Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/llama-4); [Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/artificialanalysis)
- Chart presentation: GPT-5 "mega chart screwup"/"unintentional chart crime." — [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250808-gpt-5-vibechart)
- Undefined "accuracy": Snowflake's post defines accuracy only operationally (lenient column P/R vs gold queries chosen by humans from LLM outputs; threshold unstated); the "14%" lead is ambiguous between relative and absolute. — [Snowflake](https://snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi). Databricks docs define "Good" strictly as row-value match regardless of sort/column names — a concrete definition a startup can adopt. — [Databricks docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/genie/benchmarks)
- Anonymized competitors: Genie One's rivals are "anonymized" and there is "no independent verification yet." — [Polestar](https://www.polestaranalytics.com/partners/databricks/genie); [Pebblous](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/databricks-genie-one-governed-data/en/)
- Air Street on hype dynamics: "Benchmarks with high error rates, dataset contamination, and rigged demos erode standards"; media, API providers with commercial incentives and "X influencers chasing engagement can lend false legitimacy to weak work." — [Air Street Press](https://press.airstreet.com/p/reflections-on-reflection)

### Inferences
- For a text-to-SQL/analytics launch, citing Spider/BIRD scores is now a liability, not an asset: the CIDR/PVLDB 2026 work gives any critic a ready-made rebuttal (half the labels are wrong) and Snowflake itself argued Spider is unrepresentative in 2024.

## Key Question 4: Legal/ethical constraints on benchmarking competitors' SaaS

### Takeaway
For the warehouses, the DeWitt-clause era is largely over: Snowflake's AUP now explicitly permits benchmarking and publication (with advance sharing and replication-info conditions, and a ban on publishing Preview-feature results), and Databricks adopted a reciprocal "DeWitt Embrace" clause in 2021. For LLM APIs, the operative risk is not benchmark publication but "building a competing product" clauses (Anthropic cut OpenAI's access in Aug 2025); comparative evaluation is widely described as permitted. Google BigQuery, Oracle and Microsoft still restrict publication without consent.

### Cited Findings
- Snowflake Acceptable Use Policy (Last Updated Feb 3, 2025), Section IV: "Customer may conduct benchmark tests of the Service, including the Snowflake AI Features." "With the exception of Tests involving Previews, Customer may publicly disclose, or allow third parties to publicly disclose, the results of the Test as long as" (a) "Customer shares the Test results with Snowflake reasonably in advance of its disclosure" and (b) "the disclosure includes all information necessary to replicate the Test." "Test results involving Preview features are confidential and cannot be publicly disclosed without Snowflake's prior written approval." — [Snowflake AUP](https://www.snowflake.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy/)
- Databricks (Nov 8, 2021, Justin Olsson and Reynold Xin): eliminated its DeWitt clause and adopted a "DeWitt Embrace Clause" that "invalidates the vendor's own DeWitt Clause if there's any" when a competitor benchmarks Databricks, letting Databricks "benchmark them and explicitly name them"; welcomes benchmarks done in a "transparent, fair fashion"; notes Azure and AWS adopted similar reciprocal clauses; context was Databricks' TPC-DS record (Nov 2, 2021) and Snowflake's rebuttal. — [Databricks blog](https://www.databricks.com/blog/2021/11/08/eliminating-the-dewitt-clause-for-database-benchmarking.html). The current Databricks Service Specific Terms page (Last Updated July 14, 2026) does not itself contain the benchmarking clause — it likely sits in the MCSA; verify before publishing. — [Databricks Service Specific Terms](https://www.databricks.com/legal/service-specific-terms)
- Cube's survey (updated Jun 2, 2022): Snowflake allows; Databricks/AWS (incl. Redshift) allow with reciprocity ("You may perform benchmarks or comparative tests or evaluations"); BigQuery: "Customer may only publicly disclose the results of such Tests if it..." (conditions); Oracle: no disclosure "without Oracle's prior written consent"; Microsoft SQL Server: "You must obtain Microsoft's prior written approval to disclose to a third party the results"; Cloudera, Dremio Cloud, Elastic Cloud, InfluxDB Cloud, PlanetScale prohibit; many cloud vendors forbid "abusive benchmarking" in AUPs; open-source licenses permit. The DeWitt clause is named after David DeWitt (Wisconsin Benchmark, 1982). — [Cube.dev](https://cube.dev/blog/dewitt-clause-or-can-you-benchmark-a-database)
- Anthropic Commercial Terms (Effective June 17, 2025), Section D.4: "Customer may not and must not attempt to (a) access the Services to build a competing product or service ... including to train competing AI models or resell the Services except as expressly approved by Anthropic." No clause on benchmarking or publishing evaluations was found. — [Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
- Anthropic–OpenAI (Aug 2025): Anthropic revoked OpenAI's Claude API access citing use of Claude Code in GPT-5 development as a breach of its policy against building competing products; Anthropic kept access "for limited evaluation and security purposes"; OpenAI called comparative benchmarking "standard industry practice." — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/8/anthropic-abruptly-revokes-openai-s-access-to-claude-api-over-terms-of-service-violation); [Yahoo/Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/anthropic-blocks-openai-using-claude-181524652.html)
- Spider 2.0-Snow operational dependency: the maintainers apologized (Aug 2026) for disruption because the Snowflake evaluation account was suspended; Snowflake AI Research co-authored Spider 2.0-AIFunc (465 instances on Snowflake). — [xlang-ai/Spider2](https://github.com/xlang-ai/Spider2); [Spider 2.0-AIFunc](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.06229.md)
- Leaderboard-level rules exist even where ToS allows: LMArena updated policies after Llama 4; Terminal-Bench issued integrity policies against "cheating and reward hacking" (Apr 19, 2026). — [dig.watch](https://dig.watch/updates/lmarena-tightens-rules-after-llama-4-incident); [tbench.ai news](https://www.tbench.ai/news)

### Inferences
- Benchmarking Snowflake Cortex Analyst is permitted if BOW (1) sends results to Snowflake in advance, (2) publishes everything needed to replicate, and (3) avoids any Preview-labeled feature (check Cortex Analyst's GA status per feature at run time). ## Key Question 5: Formats that worked

### Takeaway
The formats that earned durable attention were: a third-party leaderboard entry with a companion blog (Warp, Droid, Dirac), an open-source agent + harness repo with a Show HN (Augment, Refact, Dirac's 393-point Show HN), benchmark-as-product with its own news feed and CI-bearing leaderboard (Terminal-Bench/Harbor, KiloBench), cost-per-task framing (Kilo), and evals-as-customer-feature (Hex, Databricks Genie benchmarks). Internal-only comparison posts (Cortex Analyst) produced zero HN discussion.

## Key Question 7: Synthesized do's and don'ts for Bag of Words (derived from the above)

### Takeaway
Publish a reproducible, cost-aware, statistically honest comparison on a BI-realistic task set you annotate and release yourself; name competitors where ToS allows (Snowflake and Databricks do, with conditions), grade by execution results not by an LLM from a vendor you also compare, and lead with failures and reliability (pass^k), not a single headline accuracy.

### Cited Findings
(Each item cross-references the evidence above.)
- DO release harness + dataset + per-trial results + cost/latency; the Snowflake AUP makes "all information necessary to replicate" a legal condition and the community made it a credibility condition (Augment, Refact, Dirac, Terminal-Bench). — [Snowflake AUP](https://www.snowflake.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy/); [Augment](https://www.augmentcode.com/blog/1-open-source-agent-on-swe-bench-verified-by-combining-claude-3-7-and-o1)
- DO run multiple trials, report 95% CIs and paired tests; report pass@1 headline and pass^k reliability separately. — [arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00640); [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents); [arXiv 2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394)
- DO define "correct" concretely (e.g., Databricks' row-value match "regardless of sort order or column names") and publish the gold-SQL annotation process with disagreement rates, since text-to-SQL gold labels are known to be 50–66% wrong in public sets. — [Databricks docs](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/genie/benchmarks); [CIDR 2026](https://vldb.org/cidrdb/2026/text-to-sql-benchmarks-are-broken-an-in-depth-analysis-of-annotation-errors.html)
- DO pin exact model IDs, dates, settings (verbosity/thinking budgets), and scaffold; declare "no model-specific prompt tweaks" if true. — [GPT-5 System Card](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.03267); [CompileBench via Willison](https://simonwillison.net/tags/evals/)
- DO show a context-ablation (no semantic layer → semantic layer → self-improving loop) rather than claiming raw model superiority; Snowflake's own post shows the semantic model drove the gap, and MotherDuck quantified the context effect (50%→73%). — [Snowflake](https://snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi); [MotherDuck](https://motherduck.com/videos/text-to-sql-agent-evals.md)
- DO publish failure transcripts and concede where competitors win (Cursor's footnote conceding GPT-5/Sonnet 4.5; Warp admitting Opus 4 didn't help). — [Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/composer); [Warp](https://www.warp.dev/blog/terminal-bench)
- DO share results with Snowflake (required) and other vendors (courtesy) before publishing, and invite submissions/corrections. — [Snowflake AUP](https://www.snowflake.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy/)
- DO seek an independent run (Vals, Artificial Analysis, academic group) or at least publish to a third-party leaderboard where one exists. — [Latent Space: Artificial Analysis](https://latent.space/p/artificialanalysis); [Sacra: Vals](https://sacra.com/c/vals-ai)
- DON'T cite Spider/BIRD as proof; both are contaminated/mis-annotated and Snowflake already dismissed them in 2024. — [arXiv 2601.08778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08778v1); [Snowflake](https://snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi)
- DON'T grade with an LLM from a vendor whose model is also a contestant (self-preference bias), and never use a single judge for all dimensions. — [arXiv 2410.21819](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21819); [Anthropic Engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
- DON'T anonymize competitors or run them only with default settings while tuning your own; Genie One's anonymized rivals and Cortex Analyst's unnamed "Other Solution" are the examples the community ignored or doubted. — [Polestar](https://www.polestaranalytics.com/partners/databricks/genie); [Snowflake](https://snowflake.com/en/engineering-blog/cortex-analyst-text-to-sql-accuracy-bi)
- DON'T report pass@k as accuracy, hide the base model, or ship charts whose bars don't match labels (Llama 4, Composer/Kimi thread, GPT-5 chart crime). — [Interconnects](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/llama-4); [HN Algolia Cursor](https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=Cursor%20Composer&tags=story); [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250808-gpt-5-vibechart)
- DON'T claim leadership on a sub-point lead; small deltas are statistically indistinguishable and read as unconvincing. — [arXiv 2609.17394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17394); [Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/swe-bench-dead)
- DON'T publish results involving any vendor Preview feature (Snowflake forbids) — check Cortex Analyst feature status at run time. — [Snowflake AUP](https://www.snowflake.com/legal/acceptable-use-policy/)
